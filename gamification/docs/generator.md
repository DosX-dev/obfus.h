# Generator and verification model

The generator defines a reversible intermediate representation (IR), then evaluates it with an independent JavaScript model. The C emitter translates the IR separately. See [architecture](architecture.md) for module boundaries and data contracts.

## Algorithm variation

Version 5 added **Extreme**. Version 6 standardizes the third level as `hardcore` in generator inputs, IR, filenames and new saved state; its GUI label is **Hardcore**. Extreme uses 48 ASCII characters packed into 24 bounded 16-bit coordinates and 96–144 rounds. At least 32 rounds compute shared acyclic control circuits, at least 24 use sequential coupled updates, and at least 24 use unequal-partition Feistel updates. Shared circuit nodes are captured once and reused by multiple target updates. Each node feeds the next and ultimately every target; controls never read the destination bank, so inversion restores the routing before undoing the updates using the same control values. Source-dependent odd multipliers and rotation counts are genuine dependencies, not dead noise.

Groups also change coordinate encoding through fresh invertible word transformations and physical permutations. All arithmetic and predicates retain real VM execution. This creates additional recovery work; neither the round count nor VM instruction count proves a fivefold increase in human difficulty. Generated programs still use recognizable primitives and an open generator can support a general solver. No multi-DLL packaging or custom VM interpreter is introduced.

Answer envelopes and novelty history use their current storage protocol 1, independently of generator derivation. Saved answers require all four canonical difficulty keys and the current generator schema. Answers are read from authenticated storage rather than regenerated.

-   **Easy:** three to four stages over twelve bytes, keeping coordinates independently invertible. Each coordinate receives three to seven operations drawn from XOR, modular addition/subtraction, odd multiplication, rotation and left/right shift-XOR. The sequence no longer contains the same compulsory five-operation prefix. Permutations connect the stages. Shift-XOR inversion is a short finite series over the original word, not a lossy shift.
-   **Medium:** eight to twelve stages over twelve 16-bit coordinates, including four to six linked update sweeps and at least three actual functions. An update depends on an expression tree over two to four other coordinates. In addition to XOR/add/subtract, the destination can be multiplied by a source-dependent odd factor or rotated by a source-dependent count. The controlling expression excludes the destination. Reverse-order updates therefore see the original controls and invert exactly. Coupled stages are distributed through the program instead of using one compulsory linked prefix and potentially mostly independent stages afterwards.
-   **Hardcore:** 48 to 80 linked rounds over the full 256-bit block, mixing generalized Feistel with 12 to 20 sequential nonlinear rounds. Feistel chooses six to ten source coordinates, complementary destinations and fresh routing. Sequential rounds update every coordinate in a random order using expression trees over four to six other coordinates; later updates consume earlier results. These are different dependency topologies, not alternative spellings of one round. Expressions include AND/OR and input-dependent conditional branches. Critical arithmetic, lookup indexing and branch comparisons execute through real VM operations. Six-to-twelve-round groups are separated by further reversible layers.

The generator composes six additional stage families:

-   **Nonlinear substitutions:** independently generated bijective four-bit tables, rejecting affine Boolean maps. Easy uses these alongside independent arithmetic and permutations; it has three to four stages and no linked coordinates.
-   **Pair networks:** two or three randomly wired layers of disjoint pairs. Each pair now composes three to seven generated lifts rather than selecting one of three fixed formulas. Targets alternate, with a random starting direction. Each lift reads an independently mixed version of the other coordinate and applies XOR, addition, subtraction, odd multiplication or a data-dependent rotation. The source remains unchanged during that lift; inversion reverses the actual schedule and recovers the same controls.
-   **Substitution-permutation networks:** generated nonlinear substitutions interleaved with generated pair steps. Reverse the actual operation schedule, not an assumed substitution-then-network prefix. Medium always includes one of these layers alongside its four to six coupled sweeps.
-   **Data-controlled permutations:** swap two coordinates according to a predicate over a distinct third coordinate. The predicate applies a generated reversible word expression before testing a selected bit and may invert the condition. Later swaps may overwrite earlier controls; inversion must therefore run in reverse order. Medium always includes this family. Hardcore combines these layers with both round topologies.

-   **Bit-routing networks:** a full arbitrary permutation of the 8 or 16 bits in each coordinate, surrounded by generated reversible word mixes. These are not restricted to rotations or nibble permutations. Easy always combines this with arithmetic and nonlinear substitutions; the larger levels can insert it between coupled rounds.
-   **Multi-source lifting networks:** a generated schedule of destination updates controlled by two to four other coordinates. Each source receives its own reversible mix; the control accumulator combines sources through ordered XOR/add/subtract steps. The destination uses XOR/add/subtract, an odd multiplier or a nonzero rotation. Controls exclude the destination, so reversing the schedule recovers the same controls even after later updates overwrite earlier sources. Medium can select this family; Hardcore and Extreme include it and can insert further networks between round groups.

## Variation within families

Every substitution and pair receives independent reversible word-operation sequences before and after its core transformation. These sequences compose XOR, addition, subtraction, odd multiplication and rotation with independently chosen lengths, orders and constants. They participate in the result; they are not cancelling noise. Substitutions also route output nibbles through a generated permutation. SPN programs interleave substitutions with pair updates, changing when coordinates are read and written.

Substitution code can use a combined lookup, separate nibble lookups, or a generated Boolean circuit. The circuit emitter derives algebraic-normal-form coefficients from the truth table using the Boolean Mobius transform, then emits XOR/AND/shift expressions with no lookup data. All representations implement the same selected bijection; a representation change alone cannot bypass behavioral novelty admission. Controlled swaps additionally use either conditional exchange or an XOR-difference masked by the predicate, with identical behavior.

Fingerprints include operation sequences, routing, predicate forms and interleaving schedules. The recent-profile comparison also includes word-mix shapes and normalized box/pair schedules. The generator still has discoverable mathematical primitives and open construction rules; these changes reduce fixed source/machine patterns, not guarantee unrecognizability after semantic analysis.

Substitution tables are data, not generated control flow. Their permutation cycle profiles contribute to normalized shape comparison; exact table values are retained by the independent behavioral witnesses. Pair wiring and controller connections contribute to structural fingerprints. No new layer bypasses the final full-block comparison.

All arithmetic is bounded to the chosen word width. Multiplicative updates use odd factors from 1 to 15; additive pair-lift coefficients may be any integer from 1 to 15. A dependent factor is `1 + 2 * (term & 7)`. Dependent rotation counts are 1 to 8 on 16-bit words. State-mixing shift/rotation counts are nonzero and below the word width. Nibble extraction and assembly also use zero shifts, which are valid VM operations. Round expressions never multiply two unrestricted 16-bit state values. Even the largest emitted left shift, `65535 << 15`, stays within signed Windows `long`. Both architectures use the same bounded contracts.

## Composed control programs

Pair-program lengths, operation order, initial dependency direction and source-mix sequences are independently generated. Both structural fingerprints and normalized novelty profiles retain the generated program, while ignoring its numeric constants. Removing or changing a lift changes the verification algorithm rather than adding cosmetic noise. Existing difficulty budgets and mandatory round topologies remain in force.

Individual lifts may select between two different reversible operations using a generated bit of the mixed source coordinate. Inversion recomputes that predicate after reversing subsequent lifts, so it chooses the same operation without recording a branch trace. This adds genuine input-dependent algorithm changes inside the pair rather than selecting a fixed formula at generation time. Factors remain odd and at most 15; dependent rotations remain nonzero and below the word width.

Round expression trees can also compose left/right shift-XOR controls with their existing arithmetic, bitwise and conditional operations. The C emitter captures each shift-XOR operand once through a protected helper, avoiding exponential duplication of nested expressions. Hardcore and Extreme execute these controls and pair lifts through the ordinary VM arithmetic helpers. None of these changes prevents a solver from emulating the binary or learning the open construction rules; they increase the variety that a recognizer must handle.

## Difficulty admission

`src/generation/difficulty.js` evaluates a generated program before it can become a candidate. Medium must retain at least four coupled stages, three function groups and three update forms. Each output must have paths from at least half of the input coordinates. Hardcore must retain 48–80 linked rounds, including at least 24 Feistel and 12 sequential rounds, at least three different partition widths, all five update forms, real conditional expressions and paths from every input to every output. Easy cannot acquire coupled or Feistel stages. Admission failures use the ordinary deterministic retry policy; they cannot silently downgrade the level.

Dependency counts include both sides of conditional expressions and are conservative graph properties. They do not prove that every path is algebraically significant for every input, estimate human solving time, or substitute for local reverse-engineering review. Reversibility proves unique accepting input; VM execution, trace comparisons and mutation checks establish that the native implementation follows the generated program.

Structural fingerprints ignore numeric constants, passwords, library seed, function grouping, physical storage permutation, independent coordinate update order and operand order in commutative expressions. A different fingerprint is evidence of a changed operation/dependency structure, not proof of difficulty or of a mathematically different function in every possible case. This is a huge finite generator space; studying the open generator can still enable a general solver.

Each published check also has a behavioral witness: a hash of its complete outputs for three fixed valid ASCII inputs. A candidate is rejected if its structural fingerprint, normalized expression-shape fingerprint or behavioral witness has appeared before. Expression shapes discard constants, coordinate labels and independent scheduling, so renamed operands alone do not create a new task. Equal functions agree on the fixed inputs, so distinct witnesses establish different verification behavior (subject to the ordinary SHA-256 collision assumption). These witnesses do not measure reverse-engineering difficulty. Canonical sorting uses binary string order, independent of the runner's language/locale.

An additional admission gate compares the candidate's normalized operation-count profile against the last seven published checks of its difficulty. The multiset Jaccard distance must be at least 0.35 against every retained profile. New constants, coordinate names or binary hashes cannot bypass this gate. It rejects near-copies rather than merely exact duplicates. The threshold measures structural overlap, not a percentage of distinct machine instructions or solving difficulty.

## Long-term operation

The generator composes programs rather than consuming a finite list of prepared tasks. Per-coordinate operation sequences, dependent updates, expression trees, conditional branches, round partitions and routing are assembled together. This is a huge finite syntactic space, not a count of inequivalent mathematical functions. Tests exercise 36,525 daily Easy rounds, 1000 programs at every difficulty, and a century-sized history of 109,575 challenges. Different programs still share primitives and generation rules; "absolutely unrelated algorithms" is not a meaningful guarantee for an open generator.

Generator/derivation version 6 identifies the current construction rules. The answer-envelope and history protocols have their own version 1. Saved answers are decrypted and disclosed, never regenerated. Updating source does not change published programs.

The compressed release history retains all structural/behavioral/shape hashes plus normalized operation-count profiles for at most seven checks per level. Profiles omit numeric constants, coordinate labels and all passwords; they describe operation shapes rather than generated C or traces. Older profiles are dropped while their hashes remain. Existing hash-only histories remain readable and acquire the recent window as new rounds are published. Invalid or oversized profile metadata fails closed. The Git manifest contains a history count and authenticated asset references, so it does not grow by megabytes over time. The current history is loaded and checked before any new candidate is chosen. Answer encryption uses a fixed storage protocol independent of generator version changes; the next round discloses the answers actually stored by the previous publication.

Normal operation needs no manual password/seed updates or daily commits by the owner. Backup schedule runs, durable journals, deterministic retries and integrity checks handle ordinary interruptions. External service policy changes, disabled schedules, deleted archives, key loss and future Windows/runner changes can still require maintenance. Do not promise a century of unchanged GitHub infrastructure or mathematically infinite tasks. Keep the master key backed up securely. Latest compiler selection fails closed when an upstream release is unavailable or fails verification.
